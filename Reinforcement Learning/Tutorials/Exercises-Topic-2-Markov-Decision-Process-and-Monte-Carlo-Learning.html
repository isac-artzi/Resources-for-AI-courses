Computational · Value iteration versus policy iteration cost

Question 3

An MDP has 500 non-terminal states and 4 actions. Count cost in elementary backups, where one backup is a single evaluation of q(s,a).
  • Value iteration converges in 60 sweeps. Each sweep evaluates all 4 actions in every state.
  • Policy iteration converges in 5 rounds. Each round runs 12 evaluation sweeps for its current deterministic policy, then one improvement sweep over all actions.
How many elementary backups does policy iteration perform in total?
Hint · An evaluation sweep for a deterministic policy touches one action per state, not four. An improvement sweep touches all four. Cost one round, then multiply by the number of rounds.
Computational · First-visit against every-visit

Question 4

A single episode is generated under π with γ = 0.5: 
A --(r=+1)--> B --(r=-1)--> A --(r=+2)--> C --(r=+4)--> terminal
This is the only episode you have. What is the every-visit Monte Carlo estimate of V(A)? Round to two decimal places.
Hint · Work backwards with Gt = Rt+1 + γGt+1 to get the return following each of the four time steps, then average the returns following both occurrences of A.
Computational · The importance-sampling ratio

Question 5

Three actions. The target policy is π = (0.50, 0.40, 0.10) and the behaviour policy is b = (0.25, 0.25, 0.50), both the same in every state. A three-step episode is observed under b with the action sequence a₁, a₂, a₁. What is the importance-sampling ratio ρ for this episode? Round to two decimal places.
Hint · ρ = Πt π(at|st) / b(at|st). The transition probabilities appear in both the numerator and the denominator and cancel, so no knowledge of P is required.

Question 7

The Bellman optimality operator is a γ-contraction, so the max-norm residual of value iteration falls by at most a factor γ per sweep. With γ = 0.99, how many sweeps does that bound require to reduce the residual by a factor of 10⁶? Give a whole number of sweeps.
Hint · Solve γk ≤ 10−6 for k, i.e. k ≥ ln(10−6) / ln(0.99), and round up.

Question 8

Step 7 of your product asks for a 95% confidence interval across at least ten seeds. At a budget of 2,000 episodes you record the RMSE between the Monte Carlo estimate and the exact value-iteration solution for n = 10 seeds, obtaining a sample mean of 0.148 and a sample standard deviation of 0.036. Using t0.975, 9 = 2.262, what is the half-width of the 95% confidence interval for the mean RMSE? Round to three decimal places.
Hint · The half-width is t0.975, n−1 · s/√n. Note the √n, not n, and that s is the seed-to-seed standard deviation, not the standard error.
