Error Message Clinic

Score your diagnostics out of four, classify your recovery strategy, rewrite the worst, and swap compilers.

All topics  ›  Topic 2  ›  Activity 8
Format groups of threeTime 45 min

The point

A compiler is a user interface, and its users meet it almost entirely through its error messages. Dragon §4.1.3 sets the standard; this is measuring yourself against it.

Do this

  1. Collect real ones. Each person writes three broken programs — one lexical, one syntax, one semantic error — runs them through their own compiler, and pastes the actual output.
  2. Score every message out of four: does it give the LINE? name the OFFENDING THING? say what was EXPECTED? suggest a FIX? Most first drafts score one.
  3. Classify the recovery. For each syntax error, which of Dragon's four strategies did your compiler use — panic mode, phrase level, error production, or none at all?
  4. Rewrite the worst three, aiming for four out of four.
    before:  syntax error

after:   Syntax error, line 7:
           missing ';' after assignment
           suggestion: add ';' after 'total = 2 + 3'
  5. Implement one as a bison error production, and explain why the production can say more than a generic handler could.
  6. Swap compilers. Run your broken programs through a group member's. Whose messages would you rather receive at 2am?

A harder question, if you have time

Should the compiler stop after reporting an error, or keep going? Try both. Feed a file with five errors to each. Keeping going can cascade one mistake into twenty spurious ones; stopping makes the programmer recompile five times. Dragon §4.1.4 does not settle it for you.

Deliverable

A table of before/after messages with their four-point scores and the recovery strategy each used; at least one implemented error production; and a paragraph on the stop-or-continue question. This is the strongest material you can put in your Project 2 video.
