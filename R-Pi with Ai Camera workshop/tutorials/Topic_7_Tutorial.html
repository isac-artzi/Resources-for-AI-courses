Topic 7 · Multiple View Geometry and Stereo Vision

Topic 7 is the most involved topic on this hardware. A single AI Camera can't do true stereo alone, so this course uses single-camera offset capture: one camera, two shots with the camera physically shifted, geometry recovered from the image pair itself via the eight-stage Cam 8 pipeline.

Scope: camera calibration and epipolar geometry concepts, feature matching, fundamental-matrix estimation, uncalibrated rectification, disparity map computation, and depth estimation from disparity.

1. Why disparity maps require a rectified pair — and why this matters first

OpenCV's StereoBM/StereoSGBM search for matching pixels along horizontal scanlines only — they assume the two images are row-aligned. Two handheld photos never satisfy this on their own. Feed an unrectified pair straight into either algorithm and you get an image-shaped output that looks like a disparity map and is not — this fails silently, with no error message. Rectification is not an optional cleanup step; it's a required stage before disparity computation means anything at all.

The intuitive shortcut is wrong: "take two photos, run the disparity function" produces plausible-looking noise, not depth. This is the single most important fact in this topic.

2. The eight-stage single-camera offset pipeline (Cam 8)

StageWhat happens
1. CaptureTwo shots of a static scene, camera shifted between them
2. Grayscale prepConvert both to grayscale for feature matching
3. Feature detection & matchingFind and match distinctive keypoints between the two images
4. Fundamental matrix estimationRecover the epipolar geometry relating the two views from matched points
5. Uncalibrated rectificationWarp both images so matching points fall on the same horizontal row
6. Rectification checkpointHorizontal-line overlay test — verify alignment before proceeding
7. Disparity computationRun StereoSGBM on the now-rectified pair
8. Depth interpretationApply a colormap; interpret disparity as relative depth

3. Two hardware prerequisites, shared with Topic 5

Lock focus before both shots. The IMX500's focus is purely mechanical (physical tool, 20 cm–∞ range) — no software autofocus exists to shift unexpectedly, but you must set it once and not touch it between captures.
A grid/checkerboard on the whiteboard is required, not optional. Feature matching needs distinctive, well-distributed points. A blank wall or blank whiteboard yields too few features and the whole pipeline degrades regardless of how correct your code is — this is a scene problem, not a code problem, and it's easy to misdiagnose as the latter.

4. Scene and capture guidance

Success here depends more on what you photograph than on your code. The single highest-value tip: slide the camera along a table edge or a ruler rather than moving it freehand — this keeps the offset close to a pure horizontal translation, which is exactly what makes rectification and matching well-behaved.

5. Relative depth, not metric depth

This approach does not measure your camera's exact shift distance, so the resulting depth is relative — you can rank objects by distance and observe the inverse disparity-depth relationship (closer objects have larger disparity), but you cannot report distances in centimeters without an optional metric-depth extension where you measure your own camera shift.
