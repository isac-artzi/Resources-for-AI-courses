Topic 2 · Image Filtering and Enhancement

Topic 2 covers convolution-based filtering — smoothing away noise, sharpening detail, and preserving edges — plus contrast enhancement with histogram equalization and CLAHE. The camera's job here is done after Topic 1; everything in this topic is pure OpenCV work on the array you already captured.

Scope: Gaussian, Median, and Bilateral filters; convolution kernels; histogram equalization; CLAHE. All of this runs identically on any captured still — the AI Camera is simply your image source.

1. Convolution: sliding a kernel across the image

Every filter in this topic works the same way: a small matrix (the kernel) slides across the image, and at each position it computes a weighted sum of the pixels underneath it, replacing the center pixel with the result. A blur kernel averages neighbors together; a sharpen kernel amplifies the center relative to its neighbors. 

blurred = cv2.GaussianBlur(gray, (5, 5), sigmaX=1.5)
median  = cv2.medianBlur(gray, 5)
bilat   = cv2.bilateralFilter(gray, d=9, sigmaColor=75, sigmaSpace=75)

2. Gaussian vs. Median vs. Bilateral — pick the right tool

FilterBest forTrade-off
GaussianGeneral smoothing, sensor noiseBlurs edges along with noise
MedianSalt-and-pepper / speckle noiseSlower; can erode fine detail
BilateralSmoothing while preserving edgesMuch slower than the other two

A good place to see this on real hardware: the Cam 3 (Control Sweep) template sweeps ISO/gain and exposure across a range and lets you watch visible sensor noise appear at high gain — a preview of exactly the kind of noise Topic 3 (Restoration) asks you to model.

3. Contrast: histogram equalization vs. CLAHE

Histogram equalization spreads pixel intensities across the full 0–255 range using the whole image's histogram — great for globally flat, hazy images, but it can over-amplify contrast in already-bright regions. CLAHE (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization) does the same thing in small local tiles with a clip limit, avoiding that over-amplification — the standard choice for unevenly lit scenes. 

eq = cv2.equalizeHist(gray)
clahe = cv2.createCLAHE(clipLimit=2.0, tileGridSize=(8,8))
clahe_img = clahe.apply(gray)

4. Measuring the effect, not just eyeballing it

Report before/after histograms side by side, and describe the change quantitatively (e.g. standard deviation of pixel intensity, or the interquartile spread) rather than only visually. This habit — always attaching a number to a claimed improvement — is the same discipline Topic 3 asks for with PSNR and SSIM.
